# PART 1 — UNIVERSAL ILLUSTRATION DESIGN INSTRUCTION

## UNFICT PROPRIETARY ICON SYSTEM

Design **one original icon belonging to the Unfict proprietary iconography system**.

This instruction defines the permanent visual grammar of the Unfict icon family. Apply it consistently regardless of the specific function or concept assigned in Part 2.

The objective is not merely to create an attractive icon.

The objective is to create an icon that:

- communicates its intended meaning clearly;
- belongs unmistakably to the same visual family as every other Unfict icon;
- carries recognizable lineage from the canonical Unfict mark without reproducing the mark;
- remains identifiable as an Unfict-designed asset even when isolated from the wordmark, website or product;
- remains restrained enough for enterprise, infrastructure, developer, institutional and technical environments;
- can be reconstructed as production-quality SVG within the Unfict runtime contract.

The governing principle is:

> **Minimum visual distraction. Maximum recognizable character.**

Priority order:

> **SEMANTIC RECOGNITION → USABILITY → SYSTEM CONSISTENCY → UNFICT CHARACTER → DECORATIVE NOVELTY**

Decorative novelty is last.

---

## 1. BRAND CHARACTER

The icon must feel:

- precise;
- purposeful;
- calm;
- intelligent;
- contemporary;
- infrastructural;
- technically credible;
- subtly kinetic;
- visually disciplined.

It must **not** feel:

- playful;
- childish;
- bubbly;
- ornamental;
- whimsical;
- cartoonish;
- fashionable for its own sake;
- futuristic merely for visual effect;
- decorative without semantic purpose.

The intended impression is:

> **proprietary institutional infrastructure, expressed with quiet visual confidence.**

---

## 2. RECOGNIZABLE BEFORE DISTINCTIVE

The icon must first perform its semantic job.

A viewer should understand the intended function or concept as quickly as the subject permits.

Unfict character must strengthen ownership **without damaging recognition**.

For familiar universal functions, preserve the recognized visual metaphor.

For domain-specific or Unfict-specific concepts, more proprietary geometry is permitted.

Therefore:

> **The more universal the function, the less radical the customization.**

> **The more proprietary the concept, the stronger the Unfict visual grammar may become.**

Never make an icon harder to understand merely to prove that it is custom.

---

## 3. DO NOT DRAW THE UNFICT LOGO INSIDE THE ICON

The canonical Unfict mark is the **ancestor of the visual language**, not clip-art.

Do not:

- paste the canonical mark into the icon;
- paste one canonical logo fragment into every icon;
- disguise the logo as an object;
- reconstruct the logo approximately;
- distort or rotate the canonical mark;
- make every icon look like a variation of the logo itself.

Instead:

> **derive behavior, proportion, curvature, separation, directionality, terminals and negative-space logic from the mark.**

Family resemblance should be structural rather than literal.

---

# 4. UNFICT VISUAL DNA

Use the **smallest sufficient combination** of the following characteristics.

Not every characteristic must appear literally in every icon.

## 4.1 Controlled Separation

Use distinct but visually related forms when the meaning involves:

- relationship;
- transition;
- source and destination;
- input and output;
- before and after;
- bounded scope;
- transfer;
- continuity across difference.

Do not create arbitrary floating fragments.

## 4.2 The Unfict Aperture

Where appropriate, use:

> **continuous form → deliberate interruption → controlled negative space → related continuation**

The aperture must have semantic purpose.

Never force an aperture into a universal symbol if recognition becomes worse.

## 4.3 Directional Tension

When movement, transition, relation, progression, routing, exchange or transformation matters, prefer the Unfict directional family:

> **approximately 36° ± 4°**

Do not rotate entire icons unnecessarily.

Do not apply 36° mechanically.

## 4.4 Soft Aerodynamic Geometry

Combine:

- substantial but restrained visual mass;
- clean straight structures where useful;
- smooth curvature;
- controlled rounded ends;
- softened directional terminals;
- deliberate asymmetry;
- slanted directional transitions.

Forms should feel engineered but not mechanical.

Rounded geometry must feel controlled rather than cute.

## 4.5 Unfict Terminal Character

Where an endpoint exists, prefer a softened purposeful terminal that is:

- rounded;
- optically balanced;
- substantial enough to survive small sizes;
- directional where direction matters;
- integrated into the parent geometry.

Do not rely on generic round line caps as the entire brand signature.

## 4.6 Controlled Negative Space

Use negative space deliberately to establish:

- separation;
- distinction;
- hierarchy;
- continuation;
- relation;
- bounded scope;
- directional flow.

Reject micro-gaps, trapped spaces, unstable voids and accidental slivers.

## 4.7 Controlled Asymmetry

Where appropriate, prefer:

> **one dominant primary structure + one restrained secondary structure**

rather than two equally weighted decorative objects.

Asymmetry should remain optically balanced.

## 4.8 Two-Part Relational Logic

When the function naturally involves two states, entities, boundaries, objects or stages, consider two distinct but coordinated forms.

Relationship may be expressed through:

- alignment;
- directional tension;
- negative space;
- continuation;
- proximity;
- one controlled connection.

Do not automatically use literal arrows.

Use an arrow only when arrow semantics are actually required.

---

# 5. SIGNATURE RESTRAINT

For a familiar functional icon:

> use **no more than one obvious Unfict signature treatment**.

For a domain or conceptual icon:

> use **no more than two obvious signature treatments**, unless the asset is explicitly a Brand Motif.

Possible treatments:

- Unfict directional diagonal;
- Unfict terminal;
- controlled aperture;
- designed negative-space break;
- restrained two-part relationship;
- small governed Unfict Blue anchor.

The icon must never look as if branding features were added merely because space was available.

---

# 6. GEOMETRIC CONSTRUCTION

Design as a production-quality vector asset.

Canonical production frame:

> **24 × 24**

Preferred visible geometry:

> **approximately 20 × 20**

Ordinary inset:

> **approximately 2 units per side**

Optical overshoot:

> **up to approximately 0.5 unit where curves, circles or diagonals need perceptual correction**

Use optical judgment rather than mathematical centering alone.

Check:

- perceived center;
- visual weight;
- internal gap balance;
- diagonal overshoot;
- curve overshoot;
- terminal balance;
- stroke convergence;
- apparent rather than numerical symmetry.

Optical consistency wins.

---

# 7. STROKE & MASS LANGUAGE

For functional outline icons, default toward:

- approximately **2-unit stroke at the 24-grid**;
- round line caps;
- round line joins;
- consistent optical weight.

For proprietary domain icons, outline, filled or hybrid construction may be used when meaning requires it.

Avoid:

- hairlines;
- cartoon-heavy strokes;
- inconsistent weights;
- arbitrary fill/outline mixtures;
- tiny detail that disappears at normal UI sizes.

At 24 units, prefer:

- one primary silhouette;
- no more than approximately 2–3 internal ideas;
- minimal intersections;
- strong negative-space clarity.

---

# 8. SMALL-SIZE SURVIVAL

The concept must remain coherent at:

- 16 px;
- 20 px;
- 24 px;
- 32 px;
- 40 px where relevant;
- 48 px.

At smaller sizes:

- remove nonessential detail;
- enlarge critical openings;
- preserve the dominant silhouette;
- preserve the primary semantic cue;
- preserve important separation;
- avoid micro-cutouts;
- avoid tiny floating pieces;
- prevent apertures from collapsing.

A large icon that becomes ambiguous at 16–20 px is not production-ready.

A governed small-size optimized variant may be created only when optical testing proves it necessary.

---

# 9. COLOR SYSTEM

The geometry must work independently of color.

Canonical colors:

- **Deep Ink** — `#0A0D14`
- **Slate** — `#596273`
- **Unfict Blue** — `#1965EC`

Functional icons should generally be monochrome and runtime-compatible with:

```text
currentColor
```

Where Part 2 permits a governed secondary accent, use the conceptual two-channel model:

```text
PRIMARY = currentColor
ACCENT  = var(--uf-icon-accent, #1965EC)
```

Blue may act as:

- an identity signal;
- an active semantic anchor;
- an Unfict-owned structural role;
- a restrained focal point.

Do not use Blue as decoration.

Do not make every icon Blue.

Do not rely on Blue to make generic geometry look proprietary.

For ordinary two-tone domain icons, Blue should generally represent only a minority of visible mass—approximately **10–30% where appropriate**.

The test is:

> **If all color is removed, the icon must still work.**

---

# 10. SEMANTIC STATUS COLOR

Do not permanently embed red, green, yellow or orange merely because the icon represents:

- Error;
- Success;
- Warning;
- Delete;
- Unavailable;
- Processing.

Status color belongs to the surrounding design system.

The symbol must remain understandable in monochrome.

---

# 11. ORIGINALITY REQUIREMENT

Final geometry must be original.

Universal icon conventions may be studied for familiarity, but do not substantially reproduce a distinctive third-party icon design.

Do not simply:

- recolor Lucide;
- recolor Heroicons;
- recolor Material Symbols;
- recolor Phosphor;
- recolor Font Awesome;
- slightly alter a stock SVG;
- copy an existing icon and attach a Blue fragment.

The objective is:

> **familiar semantics, original geometry.**

---

# 12. VECTOR VISUAL LANGUAGE

Render as a clean vector symbol.

Use:

- flat geometry;
- precise contours;
- clean edges;
- controlled fill;
- consistent structural weight;
- deliberate negative space.

Do not use:

- gradients;
- bevels;
- gloss;
- glassmorphism;
- photorealism;
- texture;
- noise;
- painterly effects;
- extrusion;
- 3D depth;
- perspective illustration;
- dramatic lighting;
- glow;
- neon;
- drop shadows;
- decorative background scenes.

The icon is a **symbol**, not an illustration scene.

---

# 13. NO CONTAINER BY DEFAULT

Unless Part 2 explicitly requires a boundary or the semantic concept itself depends on one:

- do not place the icon inside a circle;
- do not place it inside a rounded square;
- do not add a badge;
- do not add a decorative tile;
- do not add a background plate.

The glyph itself must carry the visual identity.

A container must never compensate for weak icon design.

---

# 14. SEMANTIC DISCIPLINE

Do not strengthen meaning beyond Part 2.

Visual metaphors carry claims.

Do not introduce stronger adjacent semantics merely because they are familiar.

Examples:

- checkmark may imply verification or success;
- shield may imply protection, security or trust;
- padlock may imply authorization or privacy;
- crown may imply ownership or superior authority;
- fingerprint may imply biometric identity;
- chain may imply blockchain;
- sparkles may imply AI or magic;
- medal/seal may imply certification.

Use such metaphors only where Part 2 permits that exact meaning.

The icon must represent its exact semantic job—**not a stronger adjacent concept**.

---

# 15. PROHIBITED VISUAL SHORTCUTS

Reject concepts that become:

- generic stock iconography;
- generic SaaS iconography;
- decorative abstraction without clear semantics;
- miniature logos;
- Blue logo fragments attached to generic symbols;
- emoji;
- mascots;
- cartoon pictograms;
- cute characters;
- feature-card illustrations;
- 3D objects;
- random geometric collections;
- unnecessary network diagrams;
- node constellations;
- arbitrary technology clichés.

Also avoid by default:

- shields for trust;
- padlocks for authority;
- crowns for ownership;
- checkmarks for evidence;
- blockchain chains for receipts;
- AI sparkles for intelligence;
- QR Codes as a universal metaphor for Digital Link;
- barcodes as a universal metaphor for product identity.

---

# 16. FAMILY RECOGNITION REQUIREMENT

The icon must stand beside other Unfict icons as one coherent system.

Family resemblance should come from recurring behavior such as:

- curvature;
- terminal construction;
- optical weight;
- aperture behavior;
- controlled gaps;
- directional tension;
- asymmetry;
- mass hierarchy;
- restraint;
- negative-space quality.

Do not achieve family resemblance by repeating the same decorative fragment on every icon.

The viewer should perceive:

> **same visual language, different semantic symbol.**

Not:

> **same logo fragment attached to different objects.**

---

# 17. ISOLATED BRAND RECOGNITION GOAL

Evaluate the design under the hardest condition.

Imagine the icon shown:

- without the Unfict wordmark;
- without a caption;
- without an Unfict interface;
- without a Blue background;
- entirely in black;
- beside unrelated icon systems.

Its function should remain intelligible according to the familiarity of the concept, while the geometry still plausibly belongs to Unfict.

Target:

> **Unfict should be detectable from geometry before it is confirmed by color.**

Do not sacrifice semantic recognition to achieve this target.

---

# 18. PRODUCTION SVG COMPATIBILITY

If producing the final SVG source, comply with this canonical contract:

```svg
<svg
  xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg"
  viewBox="0 0 24 24"
  fill="none"
>
  ...
</svg>
```

Unless the governed design requires otherwise:

- omit fixed root `width` and `height` from canonical source;
- use `currentColor` for the primary foreground channel;
- use `var(--uf-icon-accent, #1965EC)` only for a governed secondary accent;
- expose no more than two public color channels;
- include no raster images;
- include no gradients;
- include no text elements;
- include no drop shadows or filters;
- remove editor metadata and hidden layers;
- keep path order deterministic;
- avoid unnecessary internal IDs;
- flatten unstable transforms where practical.

A one-channel functional icon should be compatible with inline SVG, generated components, sprites, and CSS masking.

A governed two-tone icon should prefer inline/component SVG rather than a single CSS mask.

---

# 19. CANONICAL BRAND-ASSET EXCEPTION

The canonical Unfict mark and other specifically Brand-locked assets are not ordinary `currentColor` UI icons.

Do not assume arbitrary theme recoloring is permitted.

Brand authority governs:

- approved Blue;
- approved monochrome treatment;
- approved dark-surface treatment;
- spacing;
- proportions;
- favicon/app-icon use.

---

# 20. FINAL SELF-CHECK

Before accepting the icon, evaluate all of the following.

### Semantic
- Does it communicate the Part 2 function?
- Does it avoid stronger or incorrect adjacent meanings?

### Recognition
- Is the concept as recognizable as its category reasonably permits?

### Unfict Character
- Does it visibly belong to the Unfict family?
- Is character present in geometry rather than color alone?

### Restraint
- Is every custom treatment necessary?
- Could anything be removed without losing meaning or identity?

### Originality
- Is the geometry genuinely original?

### Small Size
- Does the silhouette, gap structure and semantic cue survive 16–20 px?

### Monochrome
- Does it still work completely without Unfict Blue?

### Family Consistency
- Do curves, terminals, gaps, weight, proportions and direction follow the system?

### Runtime Readiness
- Can the design map cleanly to one or two governed color channels?
- Does it avoid embedded semantic status color?
- Can it be represented on `viewBox="0 0 24 24"`?
- Can it be consumed without product code mutating internal geometry?

### Production Readiness
- Could this be reconstructed as a clean deterministic SVG source?

If any answer is no, revise before presenting.

---

# 21. OUTPUT REQUIREMENT

Generate **one resolved icon concept**, not a collection of unrelated stylistic alternatives, unless alternatives are explicitly requested.

Show the icon:

- isolated;
- centered;
- front-facing;
- with no text inside the icon;
- with no surrounding mock interface;
- with no decorative environment;
- with no unnecessary background treatment.

Prioritize the glyph itself.

The final design must embody:

> **The logo establishes Unfict identity. The icon inherits its visual behavior.**

> **The icon should not look like the logo. It should look like it came from the same mind.**

> **Recognizable first. Proprietary second. Decorative never.**

> **One meaning. One governed geometry. One source of truth. Many valid contexts.**
