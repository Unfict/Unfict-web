# 0. Purpose

This document proposes the complete first public content system for `zyppi.me` before general product availability.

Its purpose is to place Zyppi online early enough to:

1. establish the Zyppi entity publicly;
2. begin accumulating search-engine history and topical authority;
3. seed search demand around the active Commerce / GS1 entry application;
4. create a credible institutional public presence before product launch;
5. capture early developer and implementation interest;
6. establish stable URLs that can mature rather than being discarded at launch;
7. preserve the Trojan Horse market-entry strategy;
8. avoid publicly disclosing the full internal Zyppi capability spine before the market has a reason to understand it;
9. preserve the distinction between **what Zyppi is**, **where Zyppi enters first**, and **what Zyppi is currently building**;
10. create one coherent artifact that the Council can attack before implementation.

This is not intended to be a temporary decorative “Coming Soon” splash page.

The proposed public surface is a **pre-launch authority and acquisition site**.

The governing strategic idea is:

> **Seed the market Zyppi is entering now without allowing the entry market to define the whole platform or exposing the entire castle behind the doorway.**

## 10.9 FAQ

### Is GS1 Digital Link just a QR code?

No.

A QR code is a data carrier.

GS1 Digital Link defines how GS1 identifiers and related data can be expressed in a web-resolvable form.

The carrier and the identity / linking semantics are different layers.

### Do I have to change the physical product every time a destination changes?

A central reason to use persistent resolution is to avoid binding the physical identity permanently to one changeable web destination.

The exact implementation depends on how the identity and resources are configured.

### Is Zyppi a GS1 standards body?

No.

Zyppi is not GS1 and does not define GS1 standards.

Zyppi is building infrastructure intended to interoperate with applicable GS1 standards.

### Does Zyppi replace my product information system?

No.

The preferred model is to integrate with or reference the systems that already own the relevant content and operational state.

### Is Zyppi publicly available?

Not yet.

Private preview is in development.

# 15. Insight Article 1
# What Is a GS1 Digital Link Resolver?

**Proposed slug:** `/insights/what-is-a-gs1-digital-link-resolver`

**SEO title:** What Is a GS1 Digital Link Resolver? | Zyppi

**Meta description:** Learn what a GS1 Digital Link resolver does, how it connects product identity to digital resources, and what implementation teams should consider.

---

A physical product can carry an identity for a long time.

The digital resources associated with that product may change much more frequently.

That creates a basic infrastructure problem:

> **How can a persistent product identity keep connecting to the appropriate digital resource without turning the physical carrier into a permanent hard-coded URL?**

A GS1 Digital Link resolver participates in that relationship.

## The basic model

Conceptually:

```text
PRODUCT IDENTITY
      ↓
GS1 DIGITAL LINK
      ↓
REQUEST
      ↓
RESOLVER
      ↓
RESOURCE
```

The resolver interprets the relevant identity and resource request and determines the appropriate response according to the configured standards-compatible relationships.

It is infrastructure between identity and destination.

## Why not just redirect the QR code?

A simple redirect can be useful.

But a persistent product interaction can involve more than one resource and more than one legitimate request type.

The implementation may need to distinguish between resources such as:

- product information;
- support;
- instructions;
- regulatory information;
- certification information;
- service resources;
- other defined resource relationships.

Binding a physical carrier directly to one changeable destination reduces flexibility.

A resolver allows the stable identity and the changeable resource layer to remain separate.

## A resolver is not the destination

The resolver should not need to become:

- the product website;
- the support portal;
- the PIM;
- the ERP;
- the service platform;
- the document repository.

Those systems can remain responsible for their own content and operational state.

The resolver’s role is to help the request reach the relevant resource.

## What does an implementation team need to operate?

A production resolver may involve ongoing responsibility for:

- standards behavior;
- identifier handling;
- qualifier handling;
- resource relationships;
- error behavior;
- fallback;
- availability;
- monitoring;
- maintenance;
- standards updates.

The important implementation choice is therefore not simply:

> “Can we build a redirect?”

It is:

> **“Do we want to own this resolver capability as a continuing internal infrastructure product?”**

## Hosted or self-hosted?

Both can be legitimate.

Self-hosting may make sense when:

- the organization requires direct operational control;
- it already possesses the relevant standards expertise;
- resolver infrastructure is strategically important internally;
- maintenance cost is justified.

Managed resolver infrastructure may make sense when:

- the Job is necessary but not differentiating;
- the team wants standards maintenance externalized;
- time to implementation matters;
- the organization prefers API-level integration over operating another service.

The correct choice depends on organizational requirements.

## Where Zyppi fits

Zyppi is being built around the managed-infrastructure hypothesis:

> **Make standards-native product resolution easier to adopt without forcing every implementation team to build and continuously maintain the resolver stack itself.**

The private preview is still in development.

# 16. Insight Article 2
# GS1 Digital Link vs a Normal QR Code

**Proposed slug:** `/insights/gs1-digital-link-vs-qr-code`

**SEO title:** GS1 Digital Link vs QR Code: What Is the Difference? | Zyppi

**Meta description:** A QR code is a carrier. GS1 Digital Link defines web-resolvable GS1 identity semantics. Learn why the distinction matters in implementation.

---

A QR code and GS1 Digital Link are related in many implementations.

They are not the same thing.

The easiest way to understand the distinction is to separate three layers.

```text
CARRIER
↓
IDENTITY / LINK SEMANTICS
↓
RESOURCE RESOLUTION
```

## The carrier

A QR code is a machine-readable carrier.

It can encode many different kinds of data.

The fact that something is inside a QR code does not automatically make it GS1 Digital Link.

## The identity and link semantics

GS1 Digital Link provides a standards-based way to express GS1 identifiers and related data in a web-compatible URI form.

This allows a real-world product identity to participate in web interactions without treating the identifier as merely a marketing URL.

## The resolver

A resolver can then interpret the identity and relevant request and connect it to an appropriate resource.

That resource may change over time while the product identity remains persistent.

## Why the distinction matters

If an implementation treats the QR code, the product identity, and the final destination as one inseparable object, every future destination change can become a packaging problem.

Separating the layers creates more durable infrastructure.

```text
PHYSICAL CARRIER
stays on the product

IDENTITY
remains persistent

RESOURCES
can evolve
```

## Does every QR code need GS1 Digital Link?

No.

Different Jobs have different requirements.

The point is not that every QR code should become a GS1 Digital Link implementation.

The point is that teams working with GS1 product identity should understand the difference between the carrier and the identity / resource architecture.

## Where Zyppi fits

Zyppi is starting with the infrastructure between persistent product identity and useful digital resources.

It is not being built as a generic QR marketing platform.
