# 0. Purpose

This document projects the applicable ZyUX experience doctrine onto the **public pre-launch Zyppi website**.

It exists because the production-oriented ZyUX series contains experience laws that are already highly relevant to a public authority, acquisition, and developer-preview surface.

The pre-launch website is not the full Zyppi product.

Therefore this projection SHALL NOT import production-only complexity merely because that complexity exists in ZyUX.

The objective is:

> **Apply the smallest useful subset of ZyUX required to make the pre-launch website feel unmistakably Zyppi: simple in front, precise underneath, context-aware, low-friction, progressively explanatory, and resistant to capability overexposure.**

The intended authority chain is:

```text
MARKETING / CONTENT
What may be said and what Job is being served

        ↓

ZyUX PRE-LAUNCH PROJECTION
How that Job should be experienced

        ↓

BRAND
How Zyppi should look, sound, and feel

        ↓

STITCH
How the approved experience is composed visually

        ↓

JULES
How the approved experience is implemented faithfully
```

# 4. Pre-Launch Experience Objective

The website should make the first visitor able to answer:

1. What is Zyppi doing for this Job?
2. Why does this matter?
3. How does it work at a useful level?
4. Why might I use it instead of building / maintaining the same infrastructure myself?
5. What can I do next?

The website SHOULD NOT require the visitor to answer:

- What is the full Zyppi ontology?
- What is the full Reality Sync execution pipeline?
- What are Authority / Standing / Policy internals?
- What is the future DPP / EPCIS roadmap?
- What is Host-Native architecture?
- What future domains will Zyppi enter?

before receiving value.

# 5. Pre-Launch Experience Equation

For this website, the general ZyUX experience model is projected as:

```text
VISIBLE PRE-LAUNCH EXPERIENCE
=
VISITOR INTENT
× ENTRY PAGE
× CURRENT JOB
× DISCLOSURE BOUNDARY
× REQUIRED EXPLANATION DEPTH
× PRIMARY NEXT ACTION

```

This is a design abstraction.

It is not an authorization model.

The rule is:

> **The same public website may legitimately present different depths and calls to action depending on why the visitor arrived, without becoming different Zyppi products.**

# 6A. ZyUX-002 Pre-Launch Projection

`ZyUX-002` governs organization, relationship, scope, delegation, external-party, and Authority experience.

Most of that doctrine belongs to the later production product. For the pre-launch website, only these projections are relevant.

## ORG-PX-01 — Organizational Visitor Is Not an Admin Persona by Default

A visitor evaluating Zyppi for a company SHALL NOT be treated as though they are already an organization administrator, `Owner`, tenant creator, policy administrator, or billing administrator.

The public site should first recognize the Job:

> **Evaluate whether Zyppi fits the organization’s implementation and operating model.**

The relevant next action is:

> **Discuss implementation**

not:

> **Create organization**

unless and until the real product journey supports that state.

## ORG-PX-02 — Role Labels Must Not Become Authority Claims

Public segmentation such as Developer, Solution Architect, Engineering Lead, or Organizational Evaluator exists to improve communication.

It SHALL NOT imply that a job title automatically grants Zyppi Authority.

## ORG-PX-03 — Avoid “Owner = Super Admin” Product Signaling

The pre-launch site SHALL NOT casually use `owner account`, `master owner`, `founder admin`, or similar language as universal privilege concepts.

If organization administration is later shown publicly, use precise contextual labels only when those concepts actually exist.

## ORG-PX-04 — Enterprise Value Is Scoped Control, Not a Global Identity Graph

Organizational messaging MAY communicate coexistence with existing IAM, scoped organizational control, and existing systems of work.

It SHOULD NOT lead with a global identity graph, universal employee identity, or all-company surveillance/control framing.

## ORG-PX-05 — External Evaluation Does Not Require Fake Membership

A solution provider, partner, consultant, auditor, or other external evaluator SHALL NOT be forced into a fictitious employee/member model merely to engage with Zyppi.

The public preview/contact experience should collect implementation context, not manufacture an organizational relationship.

## ORG-PX-06 — Organizational Complexity Stays Out of Pre-Launch Navigation

The public site SHALL NOT expose future production categories such as People, Roles, Permissions, Delegations, Departments, Policies, Audit, Sources, or Security merely because `ZyUX-002` proves the architecture can support them.

## ORG-PX-07 — Buyer Messaging May Emphasize Control Without Exposing the Graph

Prefer bounded outcomes such as:

> Keep existing systems and organizational control.

> Avoid turning resolver operations into another permanent internal service.

> Evaluate Zyppi without replacing your current systems of work.

Do not expose the internal organizational experience chain:

```text
SUBJECT
→ RELATIONSHIP
→ ROLE
→ AUTHORITY
→ STANDING
→ SCOPE
→ CAPABILITY
```

on the current acquisition site.


# 7. Governing Projection Invariants

The following invariants SHALL govern the design and implementation of the pre-launch site.

---

# PX-01 — Job Before Platform

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-000` Object / Context First; `ZyUX-003` Context Before Menu.

> **The visitor should encounter the immediate Job before the breadth of the Zyppi platform.**

For the current wedge, the first-contact Job is:

```text
persistent product identity
+
GS1 Digital Link
+
reliable resource resolution
```

The homepage SHALL NOT lead with:

- universal platform architecture;
- future domains;
- product-module inventory;
- internal governance primitives.

### Stitch implication

The first viewport should visually answer:

```text
What is the Job?
Why does it matter?
What can I do next?
```

before demonstrating platform breadth.

---

# PX-02 — Minimum Useful Surface

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-000` Invisible Complexity / Contextual Capability Disclosure; `ZyUX-003` Cognitive Load principle.

> **Every visible section, control, navigation item, badge, visual, and CTA consumes cognitive capacity and must earn its place.**

Do not add elements merely to make the site feel more complete.

No decorative capability inventory.

No feature grid whose primary purpose is:

> “Look how much Zyppi can eventually do.”

### Acceptance test

For every visible object, ask:

> What visitor Job does this advance?

If there is no clear answer, remove or redesign it.

---

# PX-03 — Universal Internally, Narrow Externally

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-000` Internal Universality vs External Focus; `ZyUX-003` Navigation & Marketing Boundary.

> **The website SHALL reflect the earned current product scope, not every domain or capability the architecture could theoretically support.**

Do not show navigation, cards, badges, illustrations, or roadmaps for:

- DPP;
- EPCIS;
- Healthcare;
- Pets;
- Agriculture;
- autonomous agents;
- Host-Native systems;
- future domains;

unless separately authorized by the current website content authority.

### Principle

```text
ARCHITECTURE MAY BE UNIVERSAL

PUBLIC SURFACE REMAINS CURRENT
```

---

# PX-04 — Progressive Explanation

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-000` Progressive Explanation; `ZyUX-004` Answer → Reason → Basis → Proof.

The pre-launch site SHALL organize important explanations progressively.

Project projection:

```text
LEVEL 1 — ANSWER
What is the useful proposition?

        ↓

LEVEL 2 — REASON
Why does the problem matter?

        ↓

LEVEL 3 — BASIS
How does the relevant standards / resolver relationship work?

        ↓

LEVEL 4 — TECHNICAL DEPTH
Developer illustration, article, implementation detail
```

### Example

```text
ANSWER
Persistent product identity.
Reliable GS1 Digital Link resolution.

REASON
Physical products can outlive their digital destinations.

BASIS
Carrier → Identity → Resolution → Resource

TECHNICAL DEPTH
Developer page + conceptual JSON + Insights
```

The visitor SHALL NOT be forced into Level 4 to understand Level 1.

---

# PX-05 — Answer Simply First

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-004` governing proposition.

> **The first message should be the smallest accurate useful answer.**

Do not open with explanatory caveats before the visitor understands the basic Job.

Precision still applies.

The correct sequence is:

```text
CLEAR ANSWER
→ BOUNDED EXPLANATION
→ OPTIONAL DEPTH
```

not:

```text
INTERNAL COMPLEXITY
→ DISCLAIMERS
→ TERMINOLOGY
→ EVENTUAL VALUE
```

---

# PX-06 — Never Simplify by Lying

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-004-L02`.

> **Pre-launch simplification SHALL NOT convert planned, unknown, unverified, unavailable, or incomplete states into current certainty.**

Examples:

If product access is not generally available:

Use:

> **Private preview in development.**

Do not use:

> **Available now.**

If conformance is not yet proven:

Use:

> **standards-native**

or an approved qualified claim.

Do not design:

> **Certified / fully compliant**

badges without Evidence.

---

# PX-07 — No Proof Theater

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-004` No Proof Theater.

The public website SHALL NOT use:

- decorative green shields;
- unexplained verification checkmarks;
- “100% trusted” badges;
- fake compliance seals;
- fake standards certification;
- arbitrary confidence scores;
- decorative “secure” symbols;
- blockchain-style verification graphics;
- fake evidence counts;
- trust theater.

### Design rule

A visual state may not communicate a stronger meaning than the approved text.

---

# PX-08 — No Semantic Strengthening Through Design

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-005` No Semantic Strengthening; `ZyUX-004` scoped verification doctrine.

If public copy says:

> “Designed around applicable GS1 standards.”

the design SHALL NOT transform that statement into:

> “GS1 Certified”

through iconography, badges, labels, or visual association.

If copy says:

> “Preview”

the UI SHALL NOT visually present it like a generally available production service.

---

# PX-09 — Honest Limitations Stay Visible

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-004` uncertainty / limitation doctrine; `ZyUX-005` No Semantic Weakening.

Material limitations SHALL NOT disappear because they make the page visually less clean.

Examples:

- “Private preview in development.”
- “Illustrative only — not an API contract.”
- publication-gated standards claims;
- Zyppi independence from GS1.

These limitations should be presented calmly, not buried or dramatized.

---

# PX-10 — Public Before Registration

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-000` Observe Without Account; `ZyUX-001` No Account Required When No Account Is Needed.

All public educational content SHALL remain accessible without:

- account creation;
- login;
- email wall;
- gated download;
- mandatory newsletter signup.

This includes:

- homepage;
- GS1 Digital Link page;
- Developers page public content;
- Insights;
- About;
- public legal pages.

The developer preview form is a conversion action, not a content-access gate.

---

# PX-11 — Minimum Necessary Form Friction

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-000` Minimum Necessary Onboarding; `ZyUX-001` Minimum Necessary Identity Collection.

The preview form SHOULD ask only what is necessary to:

- identify the requester;
- understand implementation relevance;
- evaluate preview fit;
- distinguish technical context.

Do not collect pre-launch fields merely because they may be useful someday.

Avoid unnecessary:

- postal address;
- phone number;
- organization size;
- revenue;
- department hierarchy;
- billing information;
- national identifiers.

### Design rule

The form should look proportionate to the Job.

It should not resemble enterprise procurement onboarding.

---

# PX-12 — Preserve Visitor Intent

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-001-L11`; `ZyUX-003-L05`.

The website should preserve the visitor’s initiating context.

Examples:

```text
GS1 ARTICLE
→ Request developer preview
→ Success
→ Return / continue with GS1 context
```

not:

```text
GS1 ARTICLE
→ Form
→ Generic marketing dead end
```

The success state SHOULD offer:

- return to the relevant page;
- continue reading;
- another directly related next step.

---

# PX-13 — One Primary Action Per Context

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-003` Action Area / Primary vs Secondary Actions.

The homepage first viewport SHALL have one visually dominant conversion action:

> **Request developer preview**

Secondary navigation may exist.

The organizational CTA:

> **Discuss implementation**

SHALL NOT receive equal hero prominence.

On `/about`, organizational evaluation may become locally more prominent because the visitor Job differs.

### Principle

```text
ACTION PRIORITY
follows current Job
```

not one universal CTA hierarchy regardless of page.

---

# PX-14 — Navigation Serves the Current GTM

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-003` No Deep Menu; Navigation & Marketing Boundary.

The initial site navigation should remain small and stable.

Approved current family:

- GS1 Digital Link
- Developers
- Insights
- About
- Request developer preview

Do not use navigation to advertise:

- unsupported products;
- future domains;
- future applications;
- internal architecture.

### Mobile implication

Mobile navigation should become simpler, not more comprehensive.

---

# PX-15 — No Deep Menu as Future-Proofing

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-003-L10`.

Do not create empty parent categories for future scale such as:

```text
Products
Solutions
Industries
Platform
Resources
Developers
Partners
Company
```

unless the current site actually needs them.

Future scale should be enabled structurally in code, not pre-exposed cognitively in navigation.

---

# PX-16 — Stable Information Locations

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-003` Consistency Principle / stable locations for high-frequency controls.

Contextuality SHALL NOT make the site unpredictable.

Across pages, visitors should learn stable patterns for:

- where the page purpose appears;
- where primary CTA appears;
- where technical depth appears;
- where status language appears;
- where related reading appears;
- where navigation lives.

Stitch should create a reusable page grammar.

---

# PX-17 — Article Experience Is Reading-First

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-003` cognitive load; `ZyUX-004` progressive explanation.

Insights SHALL prioritize:

- title;
- concise lead;
- readable body width;
- meaningful diagrams;
- technical examples;
- relevant next reading.

Avoid:

- oversized decorative sidebars;
- floating sales cards;
- multiple sticky CTAs;
- animation that interrupts reading;
- excessive table-of-contents chrome on short articles.

The reader’s Job is learning.

---

# PX-18 — Technical Depth Must Be Observable

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-000` Developer Experience priority; `ZyUX-004` developer explanation boundary.

The Developers page should provide enough observable technical shape that an engineer can judge whether Zyppi’s direction is concrete.

Current authorized form:

> one clearly labeled conceptual request/response illustration.

It must remain:

> **ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY — NOT AN API CONTRACT**

Stitch should visually distinguish:

- conceptual example;
- actual production contract.

No fake terminal theater.

No invented SDK package installation command.

---

# PX-19 — Developer Language Stays Domain-Native

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-000` Invisible Complexity; `ZyUX-005` Semantic Nativeness.

The Developers page should use:

- GS1;
- Digital Link;
- identifier;
- resource;
- resolution;
- request;
- response;

where those concepts are appropriate.

It should not require understanding:

- constitutional primitives;
- internal policy graphs;
- Authority;
- Standing;
- Z-PROF internals;
- runtime coordinates.

The interface language should reflect the Job.

---

# PX-20 — Do Not Imply Zyppi Replaces Systems of Work

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-005` Integrate, Do Not Replace / Systems of Record Remain Systems of Record.

The website SHALL NOT visually imply that Zyppi intends to replace:

- ERP;
- PIM;
- PLM;
- WMS;
- commerce platforms;
- service systems.

Avoid giant mock “Zyppi operating system” dashboards showing every enterprise function.

Where system context is illustrated, Zyppi should appear as infrastructure connecting or enabling the relevant Job.

---

# PX-21 — Current Capability, Future Depth

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-000` Progressive Use of Zyppi.

The site MAY communicate that Zyppi can deepen over time.

It SHALL do so without enumerating the hidden future stack.

Allowed pattern:

> **Start with the GS1 Job you have now. Add additional control as your product interactions evolve.**

Avoid:

```text
Resolve
→ DPP
→ EPCIS
→ Evidence
→ Policy
→ Execution
→ AI Agents
```

on the pre-launch public surface.

---

# PX-22 — Status Is Meaning, Not Decoration

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-004` Color Is Not Meaning; status-scope principles.

Status such as:

> **Private preview in development**

should be readable as text.

Do not rely only on:

- colored dots;
- green badges;
- animated pulses;
- color-coded availability.

The status should look informational, not artificially scarce.

---

# PX-23 — Color Is Never the Only Meaning

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-004` accessibility/status doctrine.

All meaningful public states require textual meaning.

Examples:

Good:

> **Preview — not generally available**

Bad:

> green dot only

Good:

> **Illustrative example**

Bad:

> gray code block with no label

---

# PX-24 — Error States Stay Near the Job

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-003` Error Navigation; `ZyUX-004` error explanation.

For form or page errors:

```text
ERROR
→ CLEAR REASON
→ LEGITIMATE NEXT ACTION
```

Example:

> **We couldn't submit your request. Try again, or contact us if the problem continues.**

Do not route a failed form to an unrelated generic error page.

Do not use vague:

> “Something went wrong.”

when a safe, useful reason is available.

---

# PX-25 — Distinguish System Failure From Product State

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-004` system failure vs uncertainty; `ZyUX-005` host failure vs trust failure.

Pre-launch examples:

- form service unavailable;
- preview not yet available;
- page not found;
- network failure.

These are different states.

The site should not present:

> “Developer preview unavailable”

when the actual issue is:

> “Form submission service failed.”

---

# PX-26 — Mobile Prioritizes Context

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-003` Mobile Navigation.

On small screens, priority becomes:

```text
PAGE JOB
↓
KEY EXPLANATION
↓
PRIMARY CTA
↓
OPTIONAL DEPTH
```

Do not preserve desktop breadth merely by stacking every decorative element vertically.

Mobile may reduce visual complexity.

It may not remove required meaning.

---

# PX-27 — Accessibility Is Part of Experience Correctness

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-001`, `ZyUX-003`, `ZyUX-004`, `ZyUX-006` accessibility sections.

The pre-launch surface should be designed so that:

- keyboard users can reach all actions;
- focus is visible;
- hierarchy is semantic;
- text can zoom;
- status is not color-only;
- form errors are understandable;
- code blocks are usable;
- mobile touch targets are adequate;
- motion can be reduced;
- diagrams have text equivalents where necessary.

A visually clean design that hides or breaks legitimate actions for assistive-technology users fails ZyUX.

---

# PX-28 — No Visual Complexity for Its Own Sake

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-000` Invisible Complexity; `ZyUX-003` cognitive load.

Avoid:

- giant animated architecture;
- layered node webs;
- multiple floating data cards;
- visually dense “platform maps”;
- animation designed primarily to signal sophistication.

Zyppi’s depth should be inferred from clarity and coherence.

---

# PX-29 — No Marketing Capability Leakage

**Source basis:** `ZyUX-003` Navigation & Marketing Boundary.

Stitch SHALL NOT invent visual sections for:

- DPP;
- compliance;
- authenticity;
- warranty;
- recalls;
- AI agents;
- Trust engine;
- policy engine;
- execution;
- Receipts;

merely because these concepts exist elsewhere in Zyppi.

If a concept is not in the approved page content, it is not a design opportunity.

---

# 15. Experience Anti-Patterns

The following are prohibited unless separately authorized.

## AP-01 — Platform Parade

A sequence of feature cards advertising future Zyppi breadth.

## AP-02 — SaaS Template Navigation

Products / Solutions / Industries / Pricing / Customers / Resources before those categories have earned content.

## AP-03 — Fake Technicality

Terminal animations, meaningless JSON rain, code used decoratively.

## AP-04 — Proof Theater

Shields, checkmarks, “trusted” badges, fake compliance icons.

## AP-05 — Forced Signup

Gating articles or technical explanation behind email capture.

## AP-06 — CTA Saturation

Every section ending in a large button.

## AP-07 — Future Capability Leakage

DPP / EPCIS / AI Agent / Host-Native / execution roadmaps.

## AP-08 — Over-Navigation

Mega menu or deeply nested menu for eight launch routes.

## AP-09 — Dead-End Form

Preview submission with no return path or relevant next step.

## AP-10 — Generic Enterprise Dashboard Mockup

A visual implying Zyppi replaces systems of work.

## AP-11 — One Green Check for Everything

Unscoped visual certainty.

## AP-12 — Content-Fit Rewrite

Changing approved content because the design composition prefers fewer words.

# 23. Definition of ZyUX-Ready Design

A design is **ZyUX-ready for pre-launch** when:

- the Job is immediately legible;
- complexity is progressively disclosed;
- public content remains public;
- the primary action is obvious;
- buyer vs developer intent is handled contextually;
- no unsupported capability is advertised;
- no proof theater exists;
- no semantic state is strengthened visually;
- preview state is explicit;
- technical depth is observable;
- navigation remains narrow;
- mobile remains Job-first;
- accessibility is designed, not retrofitted;
- the page system is consistent without becoming generic;
- Zyppi appears deep because it is clear, not because it is visually complicated.
