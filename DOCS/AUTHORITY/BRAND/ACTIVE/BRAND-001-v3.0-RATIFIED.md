# 1. Purpose

Unfict is building infrastructure intended to operate across enterprises, standards, industries, institutions, and eventually public systems.

Its identity must therefore communicate more than technological sophistication.

It must communicate:

- clarity;
- neutrality;
- permanence;
- accountability;
- institutional confidence;
- operational trustworthiness.

The brand must be credible in the same room as:

- governments;
- regulators;
- standards bodies;
- global enterprises;
- cloud and infrastructure companies;
- auditors;
- institutional investors;
- developers.

This document defines the minimum visual and verbal system required to make Unfict recognizable at that scale.

The governing design principle is:

> **Unfict should look like infrastructure before anyone is told that it is infrastructure.**

# 2. Brand Succession and Continuity

## 2.1 Ratified Brand Name Change

Effective **13 September 2026**, **Unfict** succeeds **Zyppi** as the canonical master corporate and platform brand.

# **Zyppi → Unfict**

This is a **brand succession**, not the creation of a new project, platform, protocol, constitutional system, intellectual-property corpus, or technical architecture.

All valid work created under the Zyppi name remains part of the same continuous body of work unless a later authorized instrument expressly changes its substance.

This includes, without limitation:

- constitutional documents;
- governance records;
- technical specifications;
- architecture;
- repositories and source code;
- execution models;
- domain models;
- protocols;
- evidence;
- decisions;
- receipts;
- diagrams;
- research;
- brand history;
- intellectual property;
- historical publications.

A document does not become obsolete merely because it contains the name `Zyppi`.

Historical titles, identifiers, hashes, filenames, citations, receipts, and records SHALL NOT be silently rewritten solely to replace the legacy brand name.

Where migration is required, it SHALL be explicit, attributable, and governed.

## 2.2 Canonical Brand Name

# **Unfict**

`Unfict` is the master corporate and platform brand.

Canonical pronunciation:

# **UN-fikt**

Approximate IPA:

`/ˈʌn.fɪkt/`

Use `Unfict` in normal prose.

Use `UNFICT` where an all-uppercase treatment is appropriate, including selected logo, compact identity, metadata, legal, technical, or display contexts.

Do not expand Unfict into an acronym.

Do not create a backronym.

Do not attach product-category words to the master brand merely to explain what the company is.

## 2.3 Brand Meaning

`Unfict` is a coined brand constructed around the idea:

> **UN + FICT**

Its intended conceptual territory is:

- remove what is fictitious;
- exclude what is unsupported;
- distinguish representation from invention;
- keep digital understanding accountable to Reality.

This meaning does **not** authorize the claim that Unfict manufactures truth, possesses absolute truth, or determines reality by declaration.

Reality remains authoritative.

Unfict exists to help digital systems determine what available evidence, authority, policy, and context can legitimately support before consequential action occurs.

## 2.4 Legacy Brand Treatment

`Zyppi` is the legacy master brand.

It may continue to appear where historically accurate, including:

- legacy constitutional documents;
- historical repository records;
- prior releases;
- archived websites;
- historical diagrams;
- citations;
- migration notices;
- provenance records;
- brand-transition material.

During the transition period, the following form may be used where audience continuity requires it:

> **Unfict — formerly Zyppi**

This transition form SHALL be temporary and contextual.

New canonical public material SHALL use **Unfict** as the master brand.

Do not create a permanent dual-brand system.

Do not present `Zyppi / Unfict`, `Zyppi by Unfict`, or `Unfict by Zyppi` as co-equal master brands.

## 2.5 Technical and Constitutional Naming

This standard changes the **master brand identity**.

It does not automatically rename:

- canonical technical identifiers;
- protocol names;
- constitutional constructs;
- package names;
- repository names;
- APIs;
- namespaces;
- database identifiers;
- historical filenames;
- versioned documents.

Such changes require their own authorized migration decision where needed.

Brand modernization SHALL NOT create technical or constitutional ambiguity.

## 2.6 Legal and Trademark Boundary

Ratification of Unfict as the master brand is a **brand-governance decision**.

It is not:

- a trademark registration;
- a legal opinion;
- a company incorporation;
- a finding that every jurisdiction is legally cleared;
- a transfer of intellectual property by itself.

Formal legal-entity naming, trademark filing, and jurisdiction-specific clearance remain separate legal processes.

This brand standard remains authoritative for identity and communication unless superseded by a later ratified standard.

## 2.7 Canonical Digital Identity

The intended canonical public domain is:

# **unfict.com**

It SHALL become the primary public web identity once registered, controlled, and activated by the Chair.

No alternative domain, social handle, or legacy Zyppi domain may redefine the master brand.

Legacy digital properties may be retained for continuity, redirects, provenance, defensive ownership, and historical access.

# 4. Brand Position

Unfict is not positioned visually or verbally as a startup utility.

It is positioned as **institutional digital infrastructure**.

The brand should feel appropriate for:

- an enterprise platform;
- a standards implementation;
- a regulatory interface;
- a public infrastructure system;
- a critical developer platform;
- a long-lived global institution.

The identity SHALL avoid visual language associated primarily with:

- consumer apps;
- crypto projects;
- speculative AI startups;
- gaming;
- lifestyle technology;
- growth-hacking SaaS;
- marketing technology.

---

# 5. Core Design Axiom

The visual identity is built from three primary elements:

# **White creates the room.**

# **Ink establishes authority.**

# **Blue identifies Unfict.**

Nothing else should compete with these three roles.

The result should be simple enough to disappear behind important information, but distinctive enough to remain recognizable as Unfict.

# 6. Light-First Identity

## 6.1 Mandatory Corporate Default

The canonical Unfict brand environment is **light**.

White SHALL be the primary corporate background for:

- website marketing surfaces;
- investor decks;
- enterprise proposals;
- government and institutional documents;
- press materials;
- policy reports;
- brand standards;
- public documentation;
- official correspondence;
- events and signage where practical.

The light-first system is intentional.

It communicates:

- transparency;
- openness;
- documentation;
- institutional confidence;
- regulatory clarity;
- permanence.

## 6.2 Dark Theme

Dark mode is secondary.

It may be used where functional context justifies it, including:

- developer tools;
- operational consoles;
- monitoring interfaces;
- low-light technical environments;
- selected product UI.

Dark mode SHALL NOT become the default corporate brand expression.

It SHALL NOT define Unfict's public visual identity.

# 7. Color System

## 7.1 Core Brand Palette

The corporate identity uses a deliberately small palette.

### White

**HEX:** `#FFFFFF`

Primary use:

- canonical canvas;
- corporate documents;
- website surfaces;
- presentations;
- public communications.

### Institutional Gray

**HEX:** `#F6F7F9`

Primary use:

- secondary surfaces;
- table backgrounds;
- document sections;
- code/documentation blocks;
- understated separation.

### Deep Ink

**HEX:** `#0A0D14`

Primary use:

- primary text;
- wordmark on light surfaces;
- high-authority headings;
- diagrams;
- formal UI text.

### Slate

**HEX:** `#596273`

Primary use:

- secondary text;
- metadata;
- captions;
- supporting information.

### Border Gray

**HEX:** `#DDE1E7`

Primary use:

- rules;
- table boundaries;
- containers;
- dividers;
- form controls.

### Unfict Blue

**HEX:** `#1965EC`

`#1965EC` is retained unchanged from the Zyppi identity as a deliberate visual-continuity asset.

The approved Unfict master assets establish it as the canonical Unfict brand blue.

Primary use:

- Unfict mark;
- primary actions;
- links;
- active navigation;
- selected diagram emphasis;
- key highlights.

Unfict Blue is the only required corporate accent.

## 7.2 Corporate Color Ratio

A typical Unfict corporate surface should approximately behave as:

- **70–85% White / Institutional Gray**
- **10–25% Deep Ink / Slate**
- **2–8% Unfict Blue**

These are composition guidelines, not pixel-level mandates.

The intent is restraint.

## 7.3 Semantic Colors

Semantic colors belong to product states, not to the corporate identity.

### Verified / Valid

`#16794A` or an accessibility-compliant equivalent.

### Review / Caution

`#A15C00` or an accessibility-compliant equivalent.

### Denied / Invalid

`#B42318` or an accessibility-compliant equivalent.

### Unknown / Not Evaluated

Use Slate / neutral gray.

Semantic colors SHALL NOT compete with Unfict Blue as brand colors.

## 7.4 Violet

Violet is removed from the primary brand palette.

It may be used only in specialized product visualizations where an additional categorical series is required.

It SHALL NOT be treated as a core Unfict identity color.

## 7.5 Color Meaning

Never use green merely to imply "good."

Never use red merely to imply "bad."

In Unfict systems, color must describe the actual state.

A denied Action may indicate the platform is working correctly.

Unknown is not failure.

Review is not denial.

# 8. Typography

## 8.1 Principle

Typography should feel institutional, modern, neutral, and highly legible.

Corporate typography should not call attention to itself. The proprietary Unfict wordmark is the deliberate exception: it may carry subtle, controlled distinction while remaining highly legible.

Hierarchy SHALL come primarily from:

- scale;
- whitespace;
- weight;
- placement;
- rhythm.

Not from using multiple display personalities.

## 8.2 Primary Typeface

# **IBM Plex Sans**

IBM Plex Sans is the primary Unfict typeface for Latin-script corporate and digital communication.

Use:

- Regular 400;
- Medium 500;
- SemiBold 600.

Avoid excessive use of Bold 700.

IBM Plex Sans is used for:

- headings;
- body copy;
- navigation;
- forms;
- presentations;
- reports;
- policy documents;
- interface copy;
- enterprise communication.

There is no separate display typeface.

## 8.3 Proprietary Wordmark Typography

The canonical **Unfict wordmark is custom-drawn identity artwork**.

It is not IBM Plex Sans and SHALL NOT be recreated by typesetting `Unfict` in any standard font.

Its distinctive letterform details are part of the brand asset.

The wordmark may use subtle proprietary modifications, cuts, joins, proportions, spacing, counters, or terminal treatments to make the name recognizable at first glance while preserving institutional restraint and legibility.

These details SHALL remain subtle.

The wordmark SHALL NOT become decorative, futuristic for its own sake, or difficult to read.

Where the canonical vector wordmark is required, use the approved master asset.

## 8.4 Technical Typeface

# **IBM Plex Mono**

Use only for information whose machine identity matters:

- code;
- IDs;
- hashes;
- timestamps;
- execution references;
- API paths;
- Receipt metadata;
- Evidence references;
- machine-readable values.

Do not use monospaced typography to make marketing material appear technical.

## 8.5 Arabic and International Scripts

For Arabic and scripts not adequately supported by the primary family, use a formally approved Noto-family counterpart chosen for compatibility, readability, and institutional neutrality.

Arabic SHALL NOT be treated as a decorative translation layer.

Arabic and English layouts should receive equivalent hierarchy and authority.

A future production type audit may approve a unified multilingual family if it improves consistency without reducing legibility.

A canonical Arabic rendering of the Unfict name SHALL be approved separately before institutional use at scale.

## 8.6 Hierarchy

### Display / Hero

IBM Plex Sans SemiBold

### Section Heading

IBM Plex Sans SemiBold

### Subheading

IBM Plex Sans Medium

### Body

IBM Plex Sans Regular

### Labels / Metadata

IBM Plex Sans Medium or Regular

### Technical Values

IBM Plex Mono Regular / Medium

## 8.7 Case

Use sentence case by default.

Use uppercase only for:

- compact technical labels;
- document metadata;
- short navigational categories;
- status identifiers.

Avoid long all-uppercase headings.

# 9. Logo System

## 9.1 Canonical Assets

The approved **Unfict custom wordmark and approved Unfict mark** are the canonical identity assets.

The supplied vector geometry SHALL be treated as master geometry.

Do not redraw the mark or wordmark from memory.

Do not substitute a font for the custom wordmark.

The canonical accent blue is:

**`#1965EC`**

## 9.2 Primary Light-Surface Logo

On White and Institutional Gray:

- wordmark: Deep Ink;
- mark / approved mark accent: Unfict Blue;
- separator or containment geometry: according to the approved master lockup.

This is the default corporate logo treatment.

## 9.3 Mark–Wordmark Separation Rule

The approved Unfict mark can visually resemble a leading letterform when placed directly beside the wordmark.

Therefore, when the mark is used beside the `Unfict` wordmark, the approved **containment / separating shape** SHALL be used where defined by the master lockup.

Its purpose is structural:

- distinguish the mark from the first letter of `Unfict`;
- prevent the combined lockup from reading as `uunfict`;
- preserve immediate recognition of symbol and wordmark as separate but related assets.

Do not place the mark beside the wordmark in a way that creates an unintended extra letter.

The containment shape is not decoration.

It is part of the approved lockup logic.

## 9.4 Dark-Surface Logo

On approved dark product or event surfaces:

- wordmark: White;
- mark: Unfict Blue.

This is secondary.

## 9.5 Standalone Mark

The approved Unfict mark may be used alone for:

- application icon;
- favicon;
- social avatar;
- small device icon;
- compact product context;
- verified platform identity where the full name is already established.

The mark SHALL NOT be modified to create unrelated sub-brand symbols.

## 9.6 Clearspace

Minimum clearspace around the complete logo:

**25% of logo height on all sides.**

For the standalone mark, minimum clearspace should be no less than **25% of mark width on all sides.**

More is preferred.

## 9.7 Misuse

Never:

- stretch;
- skew;
- rotate;
- outline;
- add gradients;
- add shadows;
- recolor individual mark pieces;
- rebuild the wordmark using a font;
- alter proprietary letterforms;
- place the mark directly beside the wordmark so the lockup reads as `uunfict`;
- remove an approved separator/containment shape where it is required;
- place the logo inside decorative badges;
- animate it like a cryptocurrency token;
- combine it permanently with unrelated category symbols.

# 10. Layout

## 10.1 Philosophy

# **Public infrastructure clarity.**

Layouts should feel appropriate for a standards document, an enterprise platform, or a government digital service.

## 10.2 Grid

Use a consistent grid.

For responsive web and complex layouts, a 12-column system is recommended.

For documents and presentations, use strong alignment rather than visible decorative grids.

## 10.3 Whitespace

Whitespace is structural.

Do not fill space because it exists.

A Unfict page should usually contain fewer objects than a typical SaaS marketing page.

## 10.4 Borders and Containers

Prefer thin borders and surface changes to shadows.

Standard border:

`1px #DDE1E7`

## 10.5 Radius

Corporate documents and marketing surfaces:

- `0–4px`

Product UI:

- `4–6px` where usability benefits.

Pill shapes are restricted to controls or compact statuses that require them.

## 10.6 Shadows

Avoid decorative shadows.

Use only when real elevation or interaction hierarchy requires it.

# 11. Imagery

## 11.1 Principle

# **Show Reality before technology.**

Unfict's visual world begins with the real systems that digital infrastructure must understand.

## 11.2 Preferred Photography

Use real or documentary-style images of:

- products;
- manufacturing;
- warehouses;
- logistics;
- infrastructure;
- vehicles;
- machinery;
- credentials;
- documents;
- technicians;
- retail and product interaction;
- sensors in real environments;
- enterprise operations;
- buildings and public systems.

## 11.3 Treatment

Photography should be:

- natural;
- factual;
- restrained;
- minimally processed;
- credible.

Do not apply blue overlays simply to make an image "on brand."

Human imagery is acceptable when it shows genuine interaction with real systems, products, institutions, infrastructure, or consequences.

Where photography is used as a section background, it may be full-bleed when composition, readability, and hierarchy remain controlled.

Avoid unnecessary framed-photo cards when a direct Reality-first image can carry the section more honestly.

## 11.4 Avoid

Avoid as canonical brand imagery:

- glowing AI humanoids;
- robot brains;
- holographic hands;
- floating padlocks;
- blockchain cubes;
- random connected-node webs;
- cyberpunk interfaces;
- neon data tunnels;
- glassmorphism;
- cinematic sci-fi scenes;
- generic stock-office smiles;
- handshakes;
- abstract gradients without meaning.

Conceptual imagery may be used occasionally in presentations or editorial storytelling when clearly illustrative.

It SHALL NOT define the brand.

# 12. Diagrams and Technical Graphics

## 12.1 Standard

Unfict diagrams should look credible inside:

- an RFC;
- a standards specification;
- an architecture review;
- an enterprise technical report;
- a government technical annex.

## 12.2 Visual Language

Use:

- White background;
- Deep Ink text;
- thin Border Gray connectors;
- Unfict Blue for the principal path or selected element;
- semantic colors only when actual state meaning exists.

## 12.3 Diagram Rule

Every line must mean something.

Every node must have a purpose.

Every color must have a semantic role.

If a diagram becomes more impressive but less understandable, simplify it.

## 12.4 Universal Lifecycle

When the full constitutional lifecycle is required, preserve its semantics:

`Reality → Observation → Verification → Trust → Intent → Capability Discovery → AI Orchestration → Execution → Proof → Receipt`

For public explanations, a simplified sequence may be used where meaning remains intact, for example:

`Evidence → Verify → Trust → Policy → Action → Receipt`

Do not imply that Evidence automatically equals Trust, or that Trust automatically authorizes Action.

# 13. Iconography

Icons are functional, not illustrative decoration.

Use:

- simple geometry;
- consistent stroke width;
- low visual noise;
- familiar metaphors;
- flat 2D construction.

Avoid:

- 3D icons;
- glass icons;
- cartoon styling;
- excessive shields;
- excessive checkmarks;
- decorative cybersecurity symbolism.

Where possible, labels should carry more semantic weight than icons.

# 14. Voice

## 14.1 Core Rule

# **Say exactly what is true. Nothing more. Nothing less.**

This principle is inherited unchanged from the Zyppi brand system.

It remains the governing verbal rule for Unfict.

## 14.2 Voice Traits

The public Unfict voice is defined by four traits.

### Clear

Use plain language.

Prefer the shortest accurate explanation.

### Precise

Make bounded claims.

Distinguish what is known from what is inferred.

### Calm

Do not manufacture urgency.

Do not use emotional pressure to compensate for weak proof.

### Authoritative

State established facts confidently.

Do not hedge when evidence is sufficient.

Do not exaggerate when evidence is incomplete.

## 14.3 Persuasion

Unfict may persuade.

It persuades through:

- evidence;
- consequence;
- simplicity;
- demonstration;
- operational relevance.

Not through promotional adjectives.

# 15. Language

## 15.1 Public Language First

The public brand should not require audiences to learn Unfict's internal constitutional vocabulary before understanding the value.

Prefer:

> **Unfict helps digital systems verify what they can trust before they act.**

Before:

> Unfict executes a constitutional Trust determination across canonical Subject, Object, Place, Event, Evidence, Standing, Policy, and Capability structures.

The second form belongs in formal technical contexts.

## 15.2 "Constitutional" Language

`Constitutional` remains valid inside the Unfict governance and technical corpus.

It should be minimized in broad public marketing.

Prefer public terms such as:

- governed;
- verified;
- policy-controlled;
- accountable;
- auditable.

Use `constitutional` externally only when its formal meaning matters.

## 15.3 Canonical Brand Vocabulary

The primary public vocabulary is deliberately small:

- Unfict
- Reality Sync
- reality
- evidence
- trust
- action
- verification
- accountability

Secondary enterprise vocabulary may include:

- authority;
- policy;
- capability;
- proof;
- receipt;
- governed execution.

`Unfict` may be used selectively as a brand verb in approved human-expression contexts.

Formal technical vocabulary remains available in developer and governance contexts.

## 15.4 Capitalization

Use normal English capitalization in public copy.

Formal primitives may retain capitalized form in technical or constitutional contexts.

Example:

Public:

> Verify what the evidence supports before an action is allowed.

Formal:

> Trust is computed from Evidence before Action.

# 18. Claims Discipline

Unfict SHALL NOT claim without evidence:

- that it has no competitors;
- that it is already a blue ocean;
- that every new customer automatically creates a network effect;
- that a Receipt proves an external physical event occurred;
- that a Receipt eliminates legal liability;
- that protocol status guarantees monopoly economics;
- that GS1 2027 legally requires every brand to adopt Digital Link;
- that AI cannot operate safely without Unfict;
- that Unfict is guaranteed to become global infrastructure;
- that Unfict is destined for a specific valuation;
- that Unfict determines absolute truth;
- that the brand name itself proves legal or trademark exclusivity.

Avoid promotional language such as:

- revolutionary;
- game-changing;
- disruptive;
- magical;
- groundbreaking;
- unstoppable;
- inevitable;
- world-changing;
- best-in-class.

Preferred structure:

**Fact → consequence → bounded Unfict role.**

# 20. Institutional Documents and Presentations

## 20.1 Corporate and Government Documents

Default treatment:

- White background;
- Deep Ink text;
- Unfict Blue mark;
- thin gray rules;
- no decorative cover photography unless context requires it;
- generous margins;
- low visual noise.

A Unfict document should look credible before the reader sees the logo.

## 20.2 Investor and Enterprise Decks

The canonical Unfict deck style is light.

Use:

- White background;
- large, direct statements;
- simple diagrams;
- real photography;
- black/ink numerals;
- Unfict Blue emphasis;
- limited number of objects per slide.

Avoid a default dark cinematic presentation style.

A conceptual or launch presentation may depart from this rule when justified, but the institutional deck remains the reference standard.

## 20.3 Tables

Tables should resemble formal analytical documents.

Use:

- subtle borders;
- white or gray header bands;
- left-aligned labels;
- right-aligned numerical values where appropriate;
- minimal color.

---

# 21. Accessibility

Unfict treats accessibility as infrastructure quality.

Digital applications should target WCAG 2.2 AA.

Requirements include:

- readable contrast;
- keyboard navigation;
- visible focus;
- non-color state indicators;
- reduced motion support;
- meaningful alt text;
- plain-language summaries where technical content is complex.

Light-first design SHALL NOT become low-contrast design.

---

# 22. Brand Litmus Test

Before publication, ask:

1. Is it true?
2. Is it clear without internal Unfict knowledge?
3. Does it feel institutional rather than promotional?
4. Could it credibly appear in a government, standards, or enterprise context?
5. Is White doing most of the visual work?
6. Is Unfict Blue used selectively?
7. Is any additional color actually necessary?
