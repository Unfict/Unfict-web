# 1. Purpose

Zyppi is building infrastructure intended to operate across enterprises, standards, industries, institutions, and eventually public systems.

Its identity must therefore communicate more than technological sophistication.

It must communicate:

- clarity;
- neutrality;
- permanence;
- accountability;
- institutional confidence;
- operational trustworthiness.

The brand must be credible in the same room as:

- governments;
- regulators;
- standards bodies;
- global enterprises;
- cloud and infrastructure companies;
- auditors;
- institutional investors;
- developers.

This document defines the minimum visual and verbal system required to make Zyppi recognizable at that scale.

The governing design principle is:

> **Zyppi should look like infrastructure before anyone is told that it is infrastructure.**

# 2. Brand Foundation

## 2.1 Brand Name

# **Zyppi**

Zyppi is the master corporate and platform brand.

Do not expand the name into an acronym.

Do not create a backronym.

Do not attach product-category words to the legal or master brand name.

## 2.2 Brand Expression

# **Zyppi — Reality Sync**

`Reality Sync` is the canonical brand expression inherited from NORTH STAR v7.0.

It expresses the relationship Zyppi exists to maintain between Reality and the digital systems that must understand and act upon it.

Reality remains authoritative.

Zyppi does not manufacture truth.

## 2.3 Mission

> **Enable the digital world to perceive reality, verify trust, and execute actions with confidence.**

This line is inherited from NORTH STAR v7.0 and SHALL remain the canonical mission.

## 2.4 Plain-Language Explanation

For general public and first-contact communication:

> **Zyppi helps digital systems verify what they can trust before they act.**

This is not a constitutional definition.

It is the preferred plain-language explanation.

## 2.5 Human Expression

> **Zyppify Reality. Trust what follows.**

Use selectively.

It is a memorable brand expression, not a mandatory tagline on every surface.

# 3. Brand Position

Zyppi is not positioned visually or verbally as a startup utility.

It is positioned as **institutional digital infrastructure**.

The brand should feel appropriate for:

- an enterprise platform;
- a standards implementation;
- a regulatory interface;
- a public infrastructure system;
- a critical developer platform;
- a long-lived global institution.

The identity SHALL avoid visual language associated primarily with:

- consumer apps;
- crypto projects;
- speculative AI startups;
- gaming;
- lifestyle technology;
- growth-hacking SaaS;
- marketing technology.

---

# 4. Core Design Axiom

The visual identity is built from three primary elements:

# **White creates the room.**

# **Ink establishes authority.**

# **Blue identifies Zyppi.**

Nothing else should compete with these three roles.

The result should be simple enough to disappear behind important information, but distinctive enough to remain recognizable as Zyppi.

---

# 5. Light-First Identity

## 5.1 Mandatory Corporate Default

The canonical Zyppi brand environment is **light**.

White SHALL be the primary corporate background for:

- website marketing surfaces;
- investor decks;
- enterprise proposals;
- government and institutional documents;
- press materials;
- policy reports;
- brand standards;
- public documentation;
- official correspondence;
- events and signage where practical.

The light-first system is intentional.

It communicates:

- transparency;
- openness;
- documentation;
- institutional confidence;
- regulatory clarity;
- permanence.

## 5.2 Dark Theme

Dark mode is secondary.

It may be used where functional context justifies it, including:

- developer tools;
- operational consoles;
- monitoring interfaces;
- low-light technical environments;
- selected product UI.

Dark mode SHALL NOT become the default corporate brand expression.

It SHALL NOT define Zyppi's public visual identity.

# 6. Color System

## 6.1 Core Brand Palette

The corporate identity uses a deliberately small palette.

### White

**HEX:** `#FFFFFF`

Primary use:

- canonical canvas;
- corporate documents;
- website surfaces;
- presentations;
- public communications.

### Institutional Gray

**HEX:** `#F6F7F9`

Primary use:

- secondary surfaces;
- table backgrounds;
- document sections;
- code/documentation blocks;
- understated separation.

### Deep Ink

**HEX:** `#0A0D14`

Primary use:

- primary text;
- wordmark on light surfaces;
- high-authority headings;
- diagrams;
- formal UI text.

### Slate

**HEX:** `#596273`

Primary use:

- secondary text;
- metadata;
- captions;
- supporting information.

### Border Gray

**HEX:** `#DDE1E7`

Primary use:

- rules;
- table boundaries;
- containers;
- dividers;
- form controls.

### Zyppi Blue

**HEX:** `#1965EC`

The supplied master logo files establish `#1965EC` as the canonical Zyppi brand blue.

Primary use:

- Zyppi mark;
- primary actions;
- links;
- active navigation;
- selected diagram emphasis;
- key highlights.

Zyppi Blue is the only required corporate accent.

## 6.2 Corporate Color Ratio

A typical Zyppi corporate surface should approximately behave as:

- **70–85% White / Institutional Gray**
- **10–25% Deep Ink / Slate**
- **2–8% Zyppi Blue**

These are composition guidelines, not pixel-level mandates.

The intent is restraint.

## 6.3 Semantic Colors

Semantic colors belong to product states, not to the corporate identity.

### Verified / Valid

`#16794A` or an accessibility-compliant equivalent.

### Review / Caution

`#A15C00` or an accessibility-compliant equivalent.

### Denied / Invalid

`#B42318` or an accessibility-compliant equivalent.

### Unknown / Not Evaluated

Use Slate / neutral gray.

Semantic colors SHALL NOT compete with Zyppi Blue as brand colors.

## 6.4 Violet

Violet is removed from the primary brand palette.

It may be used only in specialized product visualizations where an additional categorical series is required.

It SHALL NOT be treated as a core Zyppi identity color.

## 6.5 Color Meaning

Never use green merely to imply "good."

Never use red merely to imply "bad."

In Zyppi systems, color must describe the actual state.

A denied Action may indicate the platform is working correctly.

Unknown is not failure.

Review is not denial.

# 7. Typography

## 7.1 Principle

Typography should feel institutional, modern, neutral, and highly legible.

It should not call attention to itself.

Hierarchy SHALL come primarily from:

- scale;
- whitespace;
- weight;
- placement;
- rhythm.

Not from using multiple display personalities.

## 7.2 Primary Typeface

# **IBM Plex Sans**

IBM Plex Sans is the proposed primary Zyppi typeface for Latin-script corporate and digital communication.

Use:

- Regular 400;
- Medium 500;
- SemiBold 600.

Avoid excessive use of Bold 700.

IBM Plex Sans is used for:

- headings;
- body copy;
- navigation;
- forms;
- presentations;
- reports;
- policy documents;
- interface copy;
- enterprise communication.

There is no separate display typeface.

## 7.3 Technical Typeface

# **IBM Plex Mono**

Use only for information whose machine identity matters:

- code;
- IDs;
- hashes;
- timestamps;
- execution references;
- API paths;
- Receipt metadata;
- Evidence references;
- machine-readable values.

Do not use monospaced typography to make marketing material appear technical.

## 7.4 Arabic and International Scripts

For Arabic and scripts not adequately supported by the primary family, use a formally approved Noto-family counterpart chosen for compatibility, readability, and institutional neutrality.

Arabic SHALL NOT be treated as a decorative translation layer.

Arabic and English layouts should receive equivalent hierarchy and authority.

A future production type audit may approve a unified multilingual family if it improves consistency without reducing legibility.

## 7.5 Hierarchy

### Display / Hero

IBM Plex Sans SemiBold

### Section Heading

IBM Plex Sans SemiBold

### Subheading

IBM Plex Sans Medium

### Body

IBM Plex Sans Regular

### Labels / Metadata

IBM Plex Sans Medium or Regular

### Technical Values

IBM Plex Mono Regular / Medium

## 7.6 Case

Use sentence case by default.

Use uppercase only for:

- compact technical labels;
- document metadata;
- short navigational categories;
- status identifiers.

Avoid long all-uppercase headings.

# 8. Logo System

## 8.1 Canonical Assets

The supplied Zyppi wordmark and two-part mark are the canonical identity assets.

The supplied vector geometry SHALL be treated as master geometry.

The supplied canonical blue is:

**`#1965EC`**

## 8.2 Primary Light-Surface Logo

On White and Institutional Gray:

- wordmark: Deep Ink;
- mark: Zyppi Blue.

This is the default corporate logo treatment.

## 8.3 Dark-Surface Logo

On approved dark product or event surfaces:

- wordmark: White;
- mark: Zyppi Blue.

This is secondary.

## 8.4 Standalone Mark

The two-part Zyppi mark may be used alone for:

- application icon;
- favicon;
- social avatar;
- small device icon;
- compact product context.

The two parts SHALL remain together.

One piece SHALL NOT be used alone as a brand mark.

## 8.5 Clearspace

Minimum clearspace around the complete logo:

**25% of logo height on all sides.**

More is preferred.

## 8.6 Misuse

Never:

- stretch;
- skew;
- rotate;
- outline;
- add gradients;
- add shadows;
- recolor individual mark pieces;
- rebuild the wordmark using a font;
- place the logo inside decorative badges;
- animate it like a cryptocurrency token;
- combine it permanently with unrelated category symbols.

# 9. Layout

## 9.1 Philosophy

# **Public infrastructure clarity.**

Layouts should feel appropriate for a standards document, an enterprise platform, or a government digital service.

## 9.2 Grid

Use a consistent grid.

For responsive web and complex layouts, a 12-column system is recommended.

For documents and presentations, use strong alignment rather than visible decorative grids.

## 9.3 Whitespace

Whitespace is structural.

Do not fill space because it exists.

A Zyppi page should usually contain fewer objects than a typical SaaS marketing page.

## 9.4 Borders and Containers

Prefer thin borders and surface changes to shadows.

Standard border:

`1px #DDE1E7`

## 9.5 Radius

Corporate documents and marketing surfaces:

- `0–4px`

Product UI:

- `4–6px` where usability benefits.

Pill shapes are restricted to controls or compact statuses that require them.

## 9.6 Shadows

Avoid decorative shadows.

Use only when real elevation or interaction hierarchy requires it.

# 10. Imagery

## 10.1 Principle

# **Show Reality before technology.**

Zyppi's visual world begins with the real systems that digital infrastructure must understand.

## 10.2 Preferred Photography

Use real or documentary-style images of:

- products;
- manufacturing;
- warehouses;
- logistics;
- infrastructure;
- vehicles;
- machinery;
- credentials;
- documents;
- technicians;
- retail and product interaction;
- sensors in real environments;
- enterprise operations;
- buildings and public systems.

## 10.3 Treatment

Photography should be:

- natural;
- factual;
- restrained;
- minimally processed;
- credible.

Do not apply blue overlays simply to make an image "on brand."

## 10.4 Avoid

Avoid as canonical brand imagery:

- glowing AI humanoids;
- robot brains;
- holographic hands;
- floating padlocks;
- blockchain cubes;
- random connected-node webs;
- cyberpunk interfaces;
- neon data tunnels;
- glassmorphism;
- cinematic sci-fi scenes;
- generic stock-office smiles;
- handshakes;
- abstract gradients without meaning.

Conceptual imagery may be used occasionally in presentations or editorial storytelling when clearly illustrative.

It SHALL NOT define the brand.

# 11. Diagrams and Technical Graphics

## 11.1 Standard

Zyppi diagrams should look credible inside:

- an RFC;
- a standards specification;
- an architecture review;
- an enterprise technical report;
- a government technical annex.

## 11.2 Visual Language

Use:

- White background;
- Deep Ink text;
- thin Border Gray connectors;
- Zyppi Blue for the principal path or selected element;
- semantic colors only when actual state meaning exists.

## 11.3 Diagram Rule

Every line must mean something.

Every node must have a purpose.

Every color must have a semantic role.

If a diagram becomes more impressive but less understandable, simplify it.

## 11.4 Universal Lifecycle

When the full constitutional lifecycle is required, preserve its semantics:

`Reality → Observation → Verification → Trust → Intent → Capability Discovery → AI Orchestration → Execution → Proof → Receipt`

For public explanations, a simplified sequence may be used where meaning remains intact, for example:

`Evidence → Verify → Trust → Policy → Action → Receipt`

Do not imply that Evidence automatically equals Trust, or that Trust automatically authorizes Action.

---

# 12. Iconography

Icons are functional, not illustrative decoration.

Use:

- simple geometry;
- consistent stroke width;
- low visual noise;
- familiar metaphors;
- flat 2D construction.

Avoid:

- 3D icons;
- glass icons;
- cartoon styling;
- excessive shields;
- excessive checkmarks;
- decorative cybersecurity symbolism.

Where possible, labels should carry more semantic weight than icons.

---

# 13. Voice

## 13.1 Core Rule

# **Say exactly what is true. Nothing more. Nothing less.**

This principle is retained from the original Zyppi brand system.

## 13.2 Voice Traits

The public Zyppi voice is defined by four traits.

### Clear

Use plain language.

Prefer the shortest accurate explanation.

### Precise

Make bounded claims.

Distinguish what is known from what is inferred.

### Calm

Do not manufacture urgency.

Do not use emotional pressure to compensate for weak proof.

### Authoritative

State established facts confidently.

Do not hedge when evidence is sufficient.

Do not exaggerate when evidence is incomplete.

## 13.3 Persuasion

Zyppi may persuade.

It persuades through:

- evidence;
- consequence;
- simplicity;
- demonstration;
- operational relevance.

Not through promotional adjectives.

# 14. Language

## 14.1 Public Language First

The public brand should not require audiences to learn Zyppi's internal constitutional vocabulary before understanding the value.

Prefer:

> **Zyppi helps digital systems verify what they can trust before they act.**

Before:

> Zyppi executes a constitutional Trust determination across canonical Subject, Object, Place, Event, Evidence, Standing, Policy, and Capability structures.

The second form belongs in formal technical contexts.

## 14.2 "Constitutional" Language

`Constitutional` remains valid inside the Zyppi governance and technical corpus.

It should be minimized in broad public marketing.

Prefer public terms such as:

- governed;
- verified;
- policy-controlled;
- accountable;
- auditable.

Use `constitutional` externally only when its formal meaning matters.

## 14.3 Canonical Brand Vocabulary

The primary public vocabulary is deliberately small:

- Zyppi
- Reality Sync
- Zyppify
- reality
- evidence
- trust
- action
- verification
- accountability

Secondary enterprise vocabulary may include:

- authority;
- policy;
- capability;
- proof;
- receipt;
- governed execution.

Formal technical vocabulary remains available in developer and governance contexts.

## 14.4 Capitalization

Use normal English capitalization in public copy.

Formal primitives may retain capitalized form in technical or constitutional contexts.

Example:

Public:

> Verify what the evidence supports before an action is allowed.

Formal:

> Trust is computed from Evidence before Action.

# 16. Audience Tone

## 16.1 Government / Regulator / Standards Body

Tone:

- formal;
- neutral;
- exact;
- evidence-based;
- low promotion.

Lead with:

- interoperability;
- governance;
- auditability;
- standards alignment;
- accountability;
- bounded claims.

## 16.2 Enterprise

Tone:

- operational;
- risk-aware;
- concise;
- accountable.

Lead with:

- what action is controlled;
- what evidence is considered;
- what policy applies;
- what can be reconstructed later.

## 16.3 Developer

Tone:

- explicit;
- deterministic;
- domain-literate;
- unsurprising.

Lead with the operation and contract.

## 16.4 Investor

Tone:

- ambitious;
- disciplined;
- probabilistic;
- evidence-aware.

Present:

- wedge;
- differentiation;

- upside.

## 16.5 Public / First Contact

Tone:

- simple;
- confident;
- human;

Do not lead with the full constitutional architecture.

# 17. Claims Discipline

Zyppi SHALL NOT claim without evidence:

- that it has no competitors;
- that it is already a blue ocean;
- that every new customer automatically creates a network effect;
- that a Receipt proves an external physical event occurred;
- that a Receipt eliminates legal liability;
- that protocol status guarantees monopoly economics;
- that GS1 2027 legally requires every brand to adopt Digital Link;
- that AI cannot operate safely without Zyppi;
- that Zyppi is guaranteed to become global infrastructure;
- that Zyppi is destined for a specific valuation.

Avoid promotional language such as:

- revolutionary;
- game-changing;
- disruptive;
- magical;
- groundbreaking;
- unstoppable;
- inevitable;
- world-changing;
- best-in-class.

Preferred structure:

**Fact → consequence → bounded Zyppi role.**

# 18. Product and Website Application

## 18.1 Homepage

The first viewport should communicate only what is necessary.

Recommended hierarchy:

**Zyppi**

# **Reality Sync.**

> Digital systems increasingly act on the real world. Zyppi helps them verify what they can trust before they act.

Primary CTA.

Secondary developer or product CTA.

Do not open with:

- the Universal Reality Model;
- ten-step pipeline diagrams;
- constitutional terminology;
- multiple competing taglines.

## 18.2 Product Pages

Lead with the user problem.

Reveal deeper architecture progressively.

## 18.3 Documentation

Documentation should be visually calmer than marketing.

Use:

- white canvas;
- strong hierarchy;
- narrow text measure;
- clear tables;
- low decoration;
- direct code examples.

## 18.4 Product UI

Product interfaces may use denser information than corporate marketing.

They must preserve:

- light theme as default unless context requires otherwise;
- clear state labels;
- accessible contrast;
- semantic status colors;
- restrained surfaces.

# 19. Institutional Documents and Presentations

## 19.1 Corporate and Government Documents

Default treatment:

- White background;
- Deep Ink text;
- Zyppi Blue mark;
- thin gray rules;
- no decorative cover photography unless context requires it;
- generous margins;
- low visual noise.

A Zyppi document should look credible before the reader sees the logo.

## 19.2 Investor and Enterprise Decks

The canonical Zyppi deck style is light.

Use:

- White background;
- large, direct statements;
- simple diagrams;
- real photography;
- black/ink numerals;
- Zyppi Blue emphasis;
- limited number of objects per slide.

Avoid a default dark cinematic presentation style.

A conceptual or launch presentation may depart from this rule when justified, but the institutional deck remains the reference standard.

## 19.3 Tables

Tables should resemble formal analytical documents.

Use:

- subtle borders;
- white or gray header bands;
- left-aligned labels;
- right-aligned numerical values where appropriate;
- minimal color.

---

# 20. Accessibility

Zyppi treats accessibility as infrastructure quality.

Digital applications should target WCAG 2.2 AA.

Requirements include:

- readable contrast;
- keyboard navigation;
- visible focus;
- non-color state indicators;
- reduced motion support;
- meaningful alt text;
- plain-language summaries where technical content is complex.

Light-first design SHALL NOT become low-contrast design.

---

# 21. Brand Litmus Test

Before publication, ask:

1. Is it true?
2. Is it clear without internal Zyppi knowledge?
3. Does it feel institutional rather than promotional?
4. Could it credibly appear in a government, standards, or enterprise context?
5. Is White doing most of the visual work?
6. Is Zyppi Blue used selectively?
7. Is any additional color actually necessary?
8. Is any decorative element actually necessary?
9. Does the typography disappear behind the meaning?
10. Does the layout communicate confidence without spectacle?
11. Is a formal technical term being used only because its formal meaning matters?
12. Does the claim distinguish evidence from inference?
13. Does it avoid implying that Zyppi manufactures truth?
14. Would the same page still feel credible in five years?

If not, revise.

# 24. Final Standard

Zyppi should not need visual spectacle to appear important.

Its authority should come from clarity.

Its trustworthiness should come from restraint.

Its distinctiveness should come from the Zyppi mark, Zyppi Blue, and the consistency of the system around them.

The result should be suitable for a product website today and a government technical document twenty years from now.
